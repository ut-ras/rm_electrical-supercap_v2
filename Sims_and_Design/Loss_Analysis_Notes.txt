CONFIRMED OPERATING RANGE AND NEW SWEEP

The user confirmed a four-switch bidirectional buck-boost stage and four
parallel 110 F / 18 mOhm cells, operated from 0 to 3 V with a stated 3.1 V
ceiling. capEachRated_V=3.1 records that user-stated ceiling, not an independently
checked capacitor datasheet rating. The bank is 440 F and 4.5 mOhm.

Loss_Analysis.m now samples 0.25:0.25:3 V with an assumed 30 A current limit.
The requested 0 V endpoint is explicitly excluded from this steady-state
CCM calculation. From an initially discharged bank, a current-regulated
startup transient must establish current and account for resistive drops,
minimum pulse width and bootstrap supply. At zero open-circuit voltage the
bank has no stored energy to deliver in boost. A constant 90 W command must
not be extrapolated to zero voltage. With a 30 A limit, terminal charging
power is only 30 W at 1 V and 90 W at 3 V, before converter losses.

The script selects frequencyMode="envelope": one frequency for each
inductor, equal to the largest required frequency over sampled V/I points.
Ripple and RMS losses are then recomputed at that fixed frequency. For
30 A over 0<Vcap<=3 V, the maximum ideal ripple occurs at 3 V, so the
original 3 V sizing frequency still sets the bound. Minimum pulse and
gate timing become more restrictive at lower voltage. The default helper
loss_defaults uses "per_point" for individual calculations; either mode
is selectable. Lower-current operation needs its own current sweep.

Vcap remains LOADED TERMINAL voltage. The estimated internal mean voltage
is Vcap-IL*ESR during charge and Vcap+IL*ESR during discharge. At 30 A the
bank ESR shift is 0.135 V; zero internal voltage therefore does not mean
zero terminal voltage while charging. Interpret the user's 0-3 V range
accordingly when constructing operating points and charge termination.
The capacitor voltage screen checks both terminal and estimated internal
mean voltages, but does not predict switching ripple peaks or overshoot.

IdealDutyApproximationOK checks that the converter's estimated DC conduction
drop is <=10% of Vcap and that the inferred internal voltage is nonnegative.
This is a configurable heuristic, not a model of low-voltage startup.
Failed rows retain their ideal calculations for inspection but must not
be treated as reliable duty/frequency/loss predictions. The sweep is a
component screen, not a complete controller or startup validation.

WHAT WAS CORRECTED OR CHANGED

1. Ripple ratio means DeltaI_peak_to_peak / average IL. For ideal CCM buck:
   D = Vcap/Vbus
   fsw = Vcap*(Vbus-Vcap)/(Vbus*IL*L*rippleRatio)
   With 24 V, 3 V, 30 A and r=0.2: fsw=0.4375/L_H.
   33e-5 H = 330 uH -> 1.32576 kHz.
   33e-6 H = 33 uH -> 13.2576 kHz.
   At 120 kHz the same target requires 3.64583 uH.
   Twenty percent is a design choice, not a mandatory standard or optimum.
   This gives the minimum frequency for <=20% ripple at that point, not
   an efficiency optimum. Higher frequency reduces ripple and increases
   many switching losses. Fixed frequency must satisfy the full operating
   envelope, including inductance droop with DC bias and tolerance.

2. 90/24=3.75 A on the bus and 90/3=30 A at the capacitor are the same ideal
   power. Multiplying by eight is the conversion ratio, not extra margin.
   Actual available charging power is 90 W minus the robot's other loads.
   At lower capacitor voltage, constant power would demand more current;
   impose a current limit. During peak discharge, define peak power and
   minimum capacitor voltage separately. A single 30 A point is insufficient.

3. DeltaIpp=6 A; peak=33 A; RMS^2=30^2+6^2/12=903 A^2.
   Magnetics_Design.m uses /3 and (1+RR)*I, which treat RR as HALF ripple.
   Its RR=0.2 therefore means 40% peak-to-peak, unlike the supplied equation.
   Existing magnetics/Simulink scripts are retained; the new workflow is
   independent and does not silently retune the control model.
   Additional legacy magnetics issues to resolve before winding a core:
   total magnetic reluctance for a chosen L and N is N^2/L. A flux margin
   should lower the allowed B, e.g. Ballow=Blimit/safety_factor; putting the
   factor in the denominator of the existing reluctance expression instead
   permits higher flux. Part 3 reuses the ETD44 core_reluctance for an ETD29,
   then computes gap length from total rather than gap reluctance. Recompute
   core geometry/material reluctance and subtract it before calculating gap.
   If f_sw/10 is intended as a filter frequency in Hz, convert to angular
   frequency omega=2*pi*f before using L=1/(C*omega^2). These are separate
   magnetics/filter design issues; the loss tool does not validate those cores.

4. Inductor copper loss is Irms^2*DCR(T). Copper's assumed coefficient is
   0.00393/K relative to 25 C. DCR typical is not a guaranteed maximum.
   Core and AC winding losses cannot be inferred from L and DCR alone.
   Enter CoreLoss_W and ACWindingLoss_W for each actual f/ripple/DC bias.
   These values are only valid at the point they were obtained; a sweep
   requires updated vendor data or a frequency/flux-dependent model.
   Isat definitions, temperature derating, L droop and Irms test conditions
   vary by vendor. The workbook's Irms is at 40 C rise / 25 C ambient.

5. Two identical synchronous switches contribute one Rds weighted over a
   cycle: D*Rds+(1-D)*Rds=Rds. A four-switch converter with another held-on
   series FET contributes a second Rds. Thus 2*Ron is plausible for this
   topology, but not for every buck. Both PWM legs switching simultaneously
   need a different model. In boost discharge the same ripple expression
   applies for low-side inductor current, with low-side on-duty 1-D.
   A bootstrap-only high-side cannot remain on indefinitely: verify refresh
   behavior or provide an independent floating supply for the held switch.

6. Four identical parallel 110 F / 18 mOhm cells give 440 F and 4.5 mOhm.
   Their bank heating at this point is 903*0.0045 = 4.0635 W.
   Series/parallel counts are configurable: C=Np*Ccell/Ns, ESR=Ns*ESRcell/Np.
   Verify cell voltage rating, equal current sharing and RMS capability.
   Vcap is TERMINAL voltage. Charging terminal power is 90 W at 3 V, 30 A;
   stored power is ~85.9365 W after this ESR heating. Input bus power is
   terminal power plus converter loss (exclude bank ESR to avoid counting
   it twice). ModelledTotal includes bank ESR for end-to-end efficiency.
   At 25 C, using the original resistances, total conduction=8.5785 W.
   High-voltage bus buffer current RMS^2=IL^2*D*(1-D)+D*DeltaIpp^2/12.
   Buffer ESR is unknown until supplied. The bank is assumed to carry all
   inductor ripple; parallel ceramic/filter capacitors can redistribute it.

7. MOSFET on-resistance is not the gate-path resistance. Use Rds(on) at the
   actual gate voltage and temperature for conduction. A slow gate waveform
   cannot show that the datasheet's Rds is wrong. Ciss is gate input
   capacitance; Coss is drain output capacitance. Neither is constant over
   voltage. Qg/Qsw/Qgd and actual Vds/Id switching measurements are better
   gate-drive/switching predictors than a single Ciss threshold.

8. The NCP5109 is not an unknowable black box. The model uses its output
   resistance and peak-current figures and calculates Pgate=2*Qg*Vdrive*f
   for two toggling gates. This is gate-drive SUPPLY energy, distributed
   among the driver and gate resistances, not all MOSFET junction heating.
   Driver overhead and supply losses require separate data. The loaded
   datasheet ICC includes a specified gate load; adding it wholesale to
   Qg*V*f double counts load energy. No thermal pass is inferred here.
   Timing uses maximum output resistance (60/20 ohms) and typical peak
   currents (0.25/0.5 A), specified at 15 V; default 12 V extrapolation
   is explicitly only a screen. Typical resistance is 30/10 ohms.
   NCP5109A has independent inputs and NO built-in deadtime. The B version
   adds interlock/deadtime. The model's 100 ns is a firmware assumption,
   not proof that the A variant prevents shoot-through.

9. Gate timing compares Qg/Iplateau and ln(9)*Rgate*Ciss against a user
   fraction (default 25%) of the shortest pulse after delay/deadtime.
   It is a configurable heuristic, not a guaranteed driver limit. CissLimit
   is reported as requested, alongside the charge check. The overlap model
   uses Qsw/Iplateau or Qgd/Iplateau if Qsw is absent. The Qgd-only fallback
   misses current-rise charge. Ideal conduction plus deadtime slightly
   overcounts conduction during deadtime; this small approximation is kept
   explicit. Large transition times invalidate this first-order model.

10. Switching loss includes voltage/current overlap, reverse recovery and
    output-capacitance commutation. For identical FETs the fallback pair
    estimate is Vbus*Qoss*f, or Coss*Vbus^2*f if only constant Coss is given.
    These are estimates: charge/energy must be evaluated at actual Vbus and
    commutation conditions. Prefer Ecomm_J for the whole half bridge per
    cycle, excluding overlap/recovery, or measured whole-leg Eon_J/Eoff_J
    INCLUDING them. Eon+Eoff overrides the other switching terms to avoid
    double counting. Do not blindly enter switching times/energies measured
    with a different gate driver, current, temperature, or test circuit.
    The old 2*Coss*V^2*f alone omits overlap/recovery; its factor also depends
    on how many legs are hard switched and how capacitance is defined.
    Equal conduction and switching losses is not a general optimum rule.

11. 40 V is only a candidate rating. The default screen requires
    1.2*(26 V maximum bus + 6 V overshoot)=38.4 V. Both 26 and 6 are explicit
    assumptions, not measured limits. Check actual maximum bus, Vds ringing,
    SOA, transient/avalanche conditions, junction temperature and layout.
    The included 30 V reference intentionally fails this voltage screen.

SCOPE INTERPRETATION

tek00001.webp: displayed rise=2.087 us, fall=777.1 ns, period=4.180 us
(239.23 kHz), logic high width=1.787 us (~42.8% duty, not the buck's 12.5%).
For a single-pole settled 10-90% transition, t10-90=ln(9)*tau:
tau_rise=0.949835 us; tau_fall=0.353674 us (approximate).
The confirmed part's typical 14 nF Ciss would give apparent TOTAL gate-path
Rrise~67.85 ohm, Rfall~25.26 ohm, conditional on the RC/probing assumptions.
These are conditional RC equivalents, not extracted driver resistance or
Rds(on). Subtract external/internal gate resistance only if the simple RC
assumptions hold. Do not use 3.3 nF Coss for this calculation.

The supplied Yageo PDF describes the RL low-ohmic thick-film resistor family;
the user confirmed 0.75 ohm fitted, agreeing with the schematic's 0R75 code.
The observation that bypassing it barely changes the waveform is consistent
with a driver/gate-load limitation. In a simple RC model, bypassing 0.75 ohm
removes ln(9)*0.75*C from the 10-90% edge: only 23.1 ns at 14 nF or 29.7 ns
at 18 nF, versus the observed 2087 ns rise and 777.1 ns fall. The driver's
specified 30/10 ohm typical source/sink resistances (60/20 ohm max, at 15 V)
dominate 0.75 ohm. Nonlinear capacitance/current limiting means these are
illustrative sensitivities, not guaranteed timing improvements. The bypass
experiment does not by itself establish excessive Ciss as the sole cause.

The NCP5109 low-side output is referenced to its GND pin, not bootstrap-
floating. The high-side output is referenced to BRIDGE. Vgate-to-ground
equals Vgs only if the source is at that same ground potential; source
inductance or a different ground point can invalidate the equivalence.
A floating driver output does not make an ordinary scope probe isolated.
The user-confirmed Vgs measurement is the appropriate quantity to inspect.

The supplied schematic shows the following matching channels:
IC1: D1' -> IN_LO pin 3 -> DRV_LO pin 5 -> 0.75 ohm -> LO_DRV1.
IC2: D2' -> IN_LO pin 3 -> DRV_LO pin 5 -> 0.75 ohm -> LO_DRV2.
Use the matching primed low-side input for blue; D1/D2 are separate high-side
inputs. Pin 1 is supplied by +12 V; pin 4 is GNDPWR. Each low-side input and
its directly connected MCU output are one net, not separate logic stages.
The two different MCU outputs must not be confused with one shared net.
Probing at pin 3 relative to pin 4 checks the signal actually seen by the IC,
including any ground displacement, ringing or connection problem. This is
a diagnostic check, not a claim that normal trace delay explains the
microsecond-scale behavior. A clean 3.3 V input meets the driver's stated
2.3 V high-level threshold; 12 V VCC does not require a 12 V logic signal.

The yellow trace continues rising after the blue input falls; the first
capture also appears offset from ground. Even with Vgs confirmed, channel
identity and timing at the actual driver input need checking before
attributing everything to Ciss. Low-side debugging should first measure
VCC-to-GND at the IC pins, the same IC's IN_LO and DRV_LO, and source-to-driver
GND. The bootstrap is not the primary supply for this low-side output.
With the power stage disabled, a lower PWM frequency can help establish
settled gate high/low levels and whether the anomalous timing persists.
Measure Vds and drain current for switching-energy validation. A screenshot
does not establish settled levels, Miller behavior, propagation delay or
switching energy.
The numeric diagnostics are not used to silently calibrate all candidate
FETs. The waveform does establish that a clean, fast gate drive has not
yet been demonstrated at this frequency with the shown setup.

IMPORT FORMAT

Canonical columns use SI, as spelled in import_loss_parts.m. For inductors:
PartNumber,L_H,DCR_Ohm,Isat_A,Irms_A,CoreLoss_W,ACWindingLoss_W.
Only part number, inductance, and DCR are required. MOSFET columns are shown
in the supplied CSV; only PartNumber,Vds_V,Rds_Ohm are required. Missing
dynamic parameters produce unknown loss/timing fields, not a false pass.
Numeric vendor columns need units in headers, or cells such as '33 uH',
'4.3 mOhms', '262 nC'. Common vendor headers are recognized automatically.
Unknown/ambiguous headers or units should be renamed to canonical names.
Test-condition strings like '0.35 mOhm @ 10 V' and ranges must be split
into separate fields; they are deliberately rejected instead of guessed.
Do not mix typical/max or gate-voltage conditions without noting them.
DataVds_V, RdsVgs_V and QgVgs_V record key test conditions.

INTERPRETING RESULTS

FAIL = at least one screen failed. INCOMPLETE = no known failure but required
rating data is missing. SCREEN_ONLY = checked thresholds satisfied, NOT
thermal/safety validation. KnownLossSubtotal excludes unknown terms and
can include approximations; do not rank final designs by it alone.
ModelledTotal is NaN until every modeled loss term has data. Even a complete
model excludes PCB/cable/connector/control and leakage losses unless added.
No closed-loop temperature calculation, SOA check, EMI check, bootstrap
refresh simulation, cell balancing or DCM simulation is performed.
